# Structural-survey secondary commute targets

The `secondary_distances` stage follows the usual `configure`/`execute` structure and supplies the fitted model to primary education location assignment. It uses student observations aged 15+ in current-education codes 2–8, positive finite weights, nonnegative reported distances and domestic education departure/destination geography. Foreign study or departure origins are excluded. `DISTTOSCHOOL_AIR` is interpreted using `education_distance_ss_unit`, default `km` as confirmed by the user.

Fit weighted empirical distance CDFs for each age band (15–17, 18–23, 24–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–59, 60+) and programme. Compulsory codes 2 and 3 are pooled because their school pools are identical. Other programmes remain separate. No normal/lognormal shape or arbitrary distance cap is imposed.

For each cell, effective sample size is `(sum(weights))² / sum(weights²)`. If it is below `secondary_education_distance_minimum_effective_n` (default 50), use all ages of that same programme. A programme-wide cell still below the threshold is explicitly labelled low support. If there is no valid evidence for the programme, leave its target missing and select the nearest compatible school; do not borrow another programme's distribution or silently return to MZ distances.

Within the recipient age/programme cohort, assign midpoint quantiles `(rank + 0.5) / N` from the selected CDF. An independent MZ age/school reference draw determines ordering before diary matching, with seeded random tie breaking. Legacy pure-function callers can still supply a matched donor distance. Missing/negative donor distances receive random ranks among the observed donor distances (or random tie ranks if none are valid). The reference values and any existing donor diary are not modified.

Recipients are classified domestic secondary students aged 15+ who have an education location in the base assignment. The school selector retains the 20 compatible establishments nearest to the new target distance and its existing numerically stable distance preference. Thus target distributions follow survey evidence, but realised distances can exceed targets where compatible schools are scarce. Geography is checked in validation rather than fitted as an additional sparse conditional dimension in this version.

Location outputs retain `education_secondary_target_distance_m`, `education_secondary_distance_source` and `education_secondary_distance_effective_n`. The method is `compatible_structural_survey_distance`, or the existing nearest-compatible-school fallback when no target is supported. Children, tertiary students, nonstudents and foreign/unknown secondary study classifications keep their handling.

The fitted stage also returns `support`, a table of record counts, effective sample sizes and weighted means, to inspect pooling. Survey distances describe usual commutes; actual-day education participants and term-time residence remain interpretation limits. These targets now also condition the pooled secondary destination-zone probabilities before school choice; see `secondary.md`. They do not estimate school capacity or impose destination totals.
